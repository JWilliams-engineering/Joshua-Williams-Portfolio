South-Facing Solar Tracker Performance on a North-Facing Roof

🏆 Research Award - 1st Place
"South-Facing Solar Tracker Performance on a North-Facing Roof"
Penn State Hazleton Annual Research Symposium · Spring 2025 · First Place - Undergraduate Research Category

Project Overview

North-facing rooftop solar panels operate at a fixed suboptimal angle, losing a significant portion of potential daily energy output. This project designed, built, and tested a dual-axis tracking system to dynamically orient a PV panel toward the sun's position throughout the day. The system achieved a measured 17.5% improvement in energy yield over a fixed-angle baseline, validated through direct power output comparison under matched conditions.

My Role & Approach

Solo honors research project responsible for all design, fabrication, programming, and testing. Designed the system in Fusion 360 and 3D printed custom components for a single axis solar tracker using a linear actuator. Used Arduino code with coordinates and a real time clock to calculate sun position and control actuator movement to adjust panel angle throughout the day. Collected and analyzed power output data over multiple test days and compared performance against a fixed panel for symposium presentation.

Engineering Tradeoffs

A GPS/RTC algorithm-based tracking approach was chosen over light-dependent resistor (LDR) sensors. LDR systems are simpler but produce noisy output under cloud cover and cannot distinguish diffuse from direct irradiance. The algorithm approach delivers smooth, predictable tracking at the cost of slightly higher firmware complexity.

3D-printed structural components enabled fast iteration and low fabrication cost. For Gen 2 outdoor deployment, critical joints will be transitioned to machined aluminum for improved weather resistance and long-term durability.

Technical Learnings

This project showed how mechanical design, embedded control, and power systems are tightly coupled in real energy applications. Frame geometry drove servo torque requirements, which sized the power supply, which constrained the enclosure design - each decision directly affected the next.

Placing first at the symposium reinforced the value of presenting technical work clearly to both engineering and non-engineering audiences. Gen 2 directly addresses documented Gen 1 shortcomings including joint stiffness, weatherproofing, and structured energy output data logging.
